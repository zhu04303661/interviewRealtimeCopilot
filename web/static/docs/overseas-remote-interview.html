Interviewing for an overseas remote job from your home country

Timezone math, English small talk and an unfamiliar meeting link — what is different about remote interviews for overseas companies, and how to prepare.

The work is the same; the interview is not. You are interviewed in a second language, at an odd hour, over a meeting link you may not have used — and remote teams judge communication almost as much as skill, because communication is the job when nobody shares an office.

Before the interview

  1. Confirm the time in writing with the timezone spelled out — “Wednesday 9 AM Pacific” — never “your morning”. Overseas teams book in their timezone; write it down in yours.
  2. Test the meeting link a day early and install the desktop client rather than using the browser. From mainland China, check the link loads on your normal network; our China site (copilot.xichigroup.com.cn) downloads the app without a proxy.
  3. Write a 60-second self-introduction in English and say it out loud ten times. The first two minutes set the tone for the whole call.
  4. Learn the English names of your own work: systems, tools, metrics. These are the words you cannot paraphrase mid-sentence.
  5. Prepare two questions to ask them — how the team works across timezones, what the first month looks like. Remote teams evaluate whether you communicate like someone easy to work with.

During the interview

  • Small talk is part of the test, not noise. Have two easy topics ready — your weekend, your workspace — and answer them warmly and briefly.
  • Connection problems are normal. Keep the line ready: “Sorry, I think the connection dropped for a second — could you say that again?”
  • Slow is fine. Pause for two seconds before answering rather than filling silence — on a laggy call, pauses read as thoughtfulness.
  • Ask for the question in text if you need it — “Could you paste that in the chat?” — especially for multi-part questions.
